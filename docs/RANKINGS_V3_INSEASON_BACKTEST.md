# In-season ranking backtest

Generated by `npx tsx tools/backtest-inseason-ranking.ts`.

Seven checkpoints per season. Everything up to a checkpoint is input, everything after it is truth, which is rest-of-season 5-category value built the same two-pass way as every other board in this repo.

Three boards, differing only in how `rateValue` is built. The score on top of it is the shipped `(rateValue - replacement) * expectedRemainingGames` for all three, so the comparison is about the rate and nothing else.

| Board | unprojected player shrinks toward | shrink prior |
|---|---|---|
| old board | pool mean, an average starter's line | projected totals / 82 |
| A | the replacement-level line | projected totals / 82 |
| A+B | the replacement-level line | projected totals / ESPN projected GP |

At the four original checkpoints players with fewer than 5 games are excluded, as they were when the shipped numbers were measured. The three early checkpoints (periods 3, 7, 14) score the **whole board** with no minimum: those players are precisely the ones a pool-mean prior ruins, which is why the original four could not see problem A.

Rank correlation is measured over a common set, the top 150 by the OLD board's per-game value, for every board. Scoring each board over its own top 150 would reward one that happens to select an easier-to-rank 150 without ranking anyone better.

## What shipped

**Change A shipped. Change B did not.** Change C shipped as two guards with no behaviour change.

Per section 4 of `docs/RANKINGS_V3_1_INSEASON_SPEC.md`: "If A passes and B does not, ship A alone and report B's numbers."

### A: unprojected players start at replacement level

Passes every gate. Unprojected players in the top 150 fall by 82 to 100% at all six early checkpoints, top-150 precision rises at all six, rest-of-season rho rises at all 14 checkpoints, per-game rho rises at all 8, and the paired bootstrap never drops below 71.8%. Nothing projected moves: a projected player's score is bit-identical before and after.

### B: count availability once

Fails three of the four conditions in gate 2 and 4.

| Condition | Required | Measured |
|---|---|---|
| avg rest-of-season rho, four original checkpoints | >= 0.455 | 0.453 |
| worst single-checkpoint drop | <= 0.010 | 0.012 |
| paired bootstrap not worse at 90%+ | > 10% ahead | 19.1% ahead, ok |
| per-game rho drop | <= 0.010 | 0.017 |

Gate 3 splits. Players ESPN projects for under 60 games do move up at week 3 and week 8 in both seasons, which says the diagnosis in section 1b is right. Their mean rank error shrinks in 2025-26 (27.8 to 15.3 at week 3) and GROWS in 2024-25 (11.8 to 32.5), because the old board was already close to unbiased for them that season and removing the double count overshoots.

The likely reason B loses despite being more correct on paper: ESPN's projected game count carries 11 to 12 games of median absolute error against actual (`docs/PROJECTIONS_BACKTEST.md`). Dividing totals by it pushes that error into all five category rates, and in season that costs more than the double discount it removes. The per-game view is the tell. That view answers "how good is he when he plays", which is exactly the question `projectedRate` is built for, and it got **worse** (0.403 to 0.400). A prior that is right in principle and noisier in practice loses.

The in-season shrink prior therefore stays `projectedPerGame`, ESPN's projected totals over 82. `tests/engine/inseasonBoard.test.ts` pins it with the reason.

All twenty of the largest moves belong to unprojected players, every one of them with exactly one game. No projected player appears in that list.

Two notes on the table.

**Garland and Castle are not where section 1a found them.** The spec reports them at 153 and 154, just outside the top 150, with Porter Jr. and Dybantsa at 155 and 156. In this snapshot Porter Jr. and Dybantsa still are, and they have 0 games; Garland and Castle have since played once, which is what lifted them to 61 and 36 on the before board. The symptom is the same, the specific victims had moved on by the time the snapshot was taken.

**Davis, Young and Tatum each drop a handful of places, and that is change A working rather than a side effect of it.** The replacement level `applyRestOfSeason` subtracts is a single order statistic over the board's rate values. With 53 unprojected players wrongly bunched near the pool mean, the player sitting at index 156 was better than he should have been, so the level was too high. Correcting it lowers the level, which raises everyone's value above replacement by the same constant, which the games factor then multiplies: durable players gain more than fragile ones. So the honest waiver line makes games-remaining matter slightly more, and three injury-prone stars give back 5 to 8 places. That is the opposite direction from change B's intent, and it is measured, included in every number above, and still a net gain at all 14 checkpoints.

## Gates 5 and 6, which live in other tools

**Gate 5, expert parity: PASS.** `npx tsx tools/rank-v2-vs-experts.ts` is at parity with the updated `proto.py` (0 score mismatches, 0 rank mismatches, 666 rows) at rho **0.755**, against a floor of 0.75 and a shipped 0.758. The baseline CSV was regenerated from proto.py itself rather than hand-edited; `proto.py --csv` does it, and it reproduces the committed baseline byte for byte before change A.

The 0.003 of rho is 10 players. Those are the expert-ranked players ESPN had not projected in the Oct 12 snapshot, Jayson Tatum among them, and change A moves them down by design. One consequence is worth naming: the tool's "stars inside top 40" check now holds an unprojected star to the top 150 instead, because Tatum is unprojected in that snapshot with 16 games and lands at 47 rather than 25. The check exists to catch the legacy injury multiplier burying stars at 404 and 667, and it still does. On the live board Tatum is projected for 70 games and sits at 35.

**Gate 6, preseason untouched: PASS on the board, with a measurement correction.** `tools/backtest-preseason-board.ts` still passes all five of its own gates and the shipped board reproduces its pin (0.519 and 0.410 against a pinned 0.519 and 0.409, tolerance 0.005).

The 2025-26 blend moved from 0.455 to 0.446 and its bootstrap from 95.8% to 90.3%, and **change A is not responsible**. Two rows in `espn-history.json` carry an ESPN projection of zero production in all five categories. They are no-evidence players, the app has always kept them off the board, and this tool was scoring them anyway: they sat at exactly 0, mid-pool, pulling the single-player replacement level that `seasonValues` reads. They now get the same exclusion `applyPreseasonV3` applies. Forcing the pre-v3.1 prior with that exclusion in place reproduces 0.446 exactly, which is how we know. The correction also makes Larry's own v3 board look better (0.403 to 0.432), so the blend is giving back a little of what it was earning from noise.

Worth flagging for its own sake: a single order statistic as the replacement level is sensitive enough that two junk rows out of 381 move the board by 0.009 rho. Change A's replacement LINE deliberately averages a 20-player band instead, for exactly this reason.
